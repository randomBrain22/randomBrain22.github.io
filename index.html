Ohara, Kyoto.

Beyond the busy streets of central Kyoto, the road climbs into a quiet valley of cedar-covered hills, moss gardens and old farmhouses. Ohara feels like another pace of life — unhurried, green and deeply rooted in the seasons.

THREE SMALL REASONS TO WANDER

Temple · Garden01

Sanzen-in

A temple known for its moss garden, stone paths and small Buddhist figures tucked into the greenery. Especially atmospheric after rain.

Temple · Architecture02

Hosen-in

A compact temple where the garden is framed like a living painting. Sit quietly, take in the view and notice how the light changes.

Temple · History03

Jakko-in

A historic nunnery on the quieter edge of the valley, associated with Kenreimon-in and the end of the Heike clan.

Getting to know the valley

A softer rhythm, just beyond the city.

Ohara sits in the mountains north of Kyoto. For centuries, its temples and rural setting have made it a place for retreat and reflection. The valley is particularly loved in autumn, when the hills turn warm shades of red and gold, and in early summer, when hydrangeas brighten the temple paths.

It is not a place to rush through. Leave time for a walk between temples, a small local meal and the simple pleasure of following a quiet lane into the countryside.
